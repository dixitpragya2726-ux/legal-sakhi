THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN FROM DOMESTIC VIOLENCE ACT, 2005

================================================================
SECTION 3 — DEFINITION OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE
================================================================

For the purposes of this Act, any act, omission or commission or conduct of the
respondent shall constitute domestic violence in case it—

(a) harms or injures or endangers the health, safety, life, limb or well-being,
whether mental or physical, of the aggrieved person or tends to do so and
includes causing physical abuse, sexual abuse, verbal and emotional abuse and
economic abuse; or

(b) harasses, harms, injures or endangers the aggrieved person with a view to
coerce her or any other person related to her to meet any unlawful demand for
any dowry or other property or valuable security; or

(c) has the effect of threatening the aggrieved person or any person related to
her by any conduct mentioned in clause (a) or clause (b); or

(d) otherwise injures or causes harm, whether physical or mental, to the
aggrieved person.

Explanation I — For the purposes of this section:
(i) "physical abuse" means any act or conduct causing bodily pain, harm, or
danger to life, limb, or health, and includes assault, criminal intimidation
and criminal force;
(ii) "sexual abuse" includes any conduct of a sexual nature that abuses,
humiliates, degrades or otherwise violates the dignity of a woman;
(iii) "verbal and emotional abuse" includes insults, ridicule, humiliation,
name-calling (including regarding not having a child or a male child), and
repeated threats to cause physical pain to any person the aggrieved person is
interested in;
(iv) "economic abuse" includes deprivation of economic/financial resources or
necessities the aggrieved person is entitled to, disposal of household assets
or shared property, and restriction of access to shared household resources.

Explanation II — In determining whether conduct constitutes "domestic
violence" under this section, the overall facts and circumstances of the case
shall be taken into consideration.

================================================================
SECTION 12 — APPLICATION TO MAGISTRATE
================================================================

(1) An aggrieved person, a Protection Officer, or any other person on behalf
of the aggrieved person may present an application to the Magistrate seeking
one or more reliefs under this Act. The Magistrate shall consider any domestic
incident report received from the Protection Officer or service provider
before passing an order.

(2) The relief sought may include compensation or damages, without prejudice
to the right to separately institute a suit for compensation.

(3) Every application shall be in the prescribed form.

(4) The Magistrate shall fix the first hearing date, ordinarily within three
days of receiving the application.

(5) The Magistrate shall endeavour to dispose of the application within sixty
days from the date of the first hearing.

================================================================
SECTION 17 — RIGHT TO RESIDE IN A SHARED HOUSEHOLD
================================================================

(1) Every woman in a domestic relationship has the right to reside in the
shared household, regardless of whether she has any right, title, or
beneficial interest in it.

(2) The aggrieved person shall not be evicted or excluded from the shared
household, or any part of it, except in accordance with the procedure
established by law.

================================================================
SECTION 19 — RESIDENCE ORDERS
================================================================

(1) While disposing of an application under Section 12, the Magistrate, on
being satisfied that domestic violence has taken place, may pass a residence
order:
(a) restraining the respondent from dispossessing or disturbing the aggrieved
person's possession of the shared household;
(b) directing the respondent to remove himself from the shared household
(this clause cannot be used against a woman respondent);
(c) restraining the respondent or his relatives from entering the portion of
the household where the aggrieved person resides;
(d) restraining the respondent from alienating, disposing of, or encumbering
the shared household;
(e) restraining the respondent from renouncing his rights in the household
except with the Magistrate's leave;
(f) directing the respondent to secure alternate accommodation of the same
standard, or pay rent for it, if required.

(2) The Magistrate may impose additional conditions necessary to protect the
safety of the aggrieved person or her children.

(3) The Magistrate may require the respondent to execute a bond (with or
without sureties) to prevent further domestic violence.

(4)-(7) Such orders are enforceable through the local police station, which
may be directed to assist in implementation. The Magistrate may impose
obligations on the respondent regarding rent and other payments, considering
both parties' financial needs and resources.

(8) The Magistrate may direct the respondent to return the aggrieved person's
stridhan or other property/valuable security she is entitled to.

================================================================
SECTION 18 — PROTECTION ORDERS
================================================================

After giving both the aggrieved person and the respondent an opportunity to
be heard, and on being prima facie satisfied that domestic violence has
taken place or is likely to take place, the Magistrate may pass a protection
order prohibiting the respondent from:
(a) committing any act of domestic violence;
(b) aiding or abetting the commission of acts of domestic violence;
(c) entering the aggrieved person's place of employment, school, or any
other place the aggrieved person frequents;
(d) attempting to communicate with the aggrieved person in any form,
including personal, oral, written, electronic, or telephonic contact;
(e) alienating any assets, bank accounts, or valuables used by both parties
or jointly held, without the Magistrate's leave;
(f) causing violence to the dependants, other relatives, or any person who
assists the aggrieved person;
(g) committing any other act as specified in the protection order.

Violating a protection order is a punishable offence under Section 31 —
imprisonment up to one year, fine up to Rs. 20,000, or both. This offence is
cognizable and non-bailable.

================================================================
SECTION 20 — MONETARY RELIEF
================================================================

(1) While disposing of an application under Section 12, the Magistrate may
direct the respondent to pay monetary relief to meet expenses incurred and
losses suffered by the aggrieved person (and any child), as a result of the
domestic violence. This may include:
(a) loss of earnings;
(b) medical expenses;
(c) loss caused due to destruction, damage, or removal of any property from
the control of the aggrieved person; and
(d) maintenance for the aggrieved person and her children, which may be in
addition to any maintenance order under other laws.

(2) The monetary relief must be adequate, fair, reasonable, and consistent
with the standard of living the aggrieved person was accustomed to.

(3) The Magistrate has the power to order the respondent's employer or debtor
to directly pay the aggrieved person, or deposit with the court, a portion of
wages or debt owed to the respondent, if the Magistrate so directs.

(4)-(6) If the respondent fails to make the payment, the Magistrate may direct
the respondent's employer or debtor to pay the amount directly. Failure to
pay may be enforced as an order of a court under the Code of Criminal
Procedure.
