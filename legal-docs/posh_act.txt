THE SEXUAL HARASSMENT OF WOMEN AT WORKPLACE
(PREVENTION, PROHIBITION AND REDRESSAL) ACT, 2013

================================================================
SECTION 2 — DEFINITIONS
================================================================

(a) "aggrieved woman" — in relation to a workplace, a woman of any age,
whether employed or not, who alleges to have been subjected to sexual
harassment by the respondent; in relation to a dwelling place, a woman of any
age employed there.

(n) "sexual harassment" includes any one or more unwelcome acts or behaviour
(directly or by implication), namely: physical contact and advances; a
demand or request for sexual favours; making sexually coloured remarks;
showing pornography; or any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal
conduct of a sexual nature.

(o) "workplace" includes government and private organisations, hospitals,
sports venues, any place visited during the course of employment (including
employer-provided transport), and dwelling places employing domestic
workers.

(h)-(l) "Internal Committee" and "Local Committee" refer to the committees
constituted under Sections 4 and 6 respectively. "Presiding Officer" and
"Chairperson" refer to the heads of those committees. "Respondent" means the
person against whom a complaint is made under Section 9.

================================================================
SECTION 3 — PREVENTION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT
================================================================

(1) No woman shall be subjected to sexual harassment at any workplace.

(2) The following circumstances, among others, if connected with an act of
sexual harassment, may amount to sexual harassment:
(i) implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment in her employment;
(ii) implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment in her employment;
(iii) implied or explicit threat about her present or future employment
status;
(iv) interference with her work or creating an intimidating, offensive, or
hostile work environment;
(v) humiliating treatment likely to affect her health or safety.

================================================================
SECTION 4 — CONSTITUTION OF INTERNAL COMPLAINTS COMMITTEE (IC)
================================================================

(1) Every employer shall, by written order, constitute an Internal Complaints
Committee (IC). Where offices are at different locations, an IC shall be
constituted at each administrative unit.

(2) The IC shall consist of:
(a) a Presiding Officer — a woman employed at a senior level;
(b) at least two Members from among employees with social work or legal
experience;
(c) one Member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women.
At least half of the nominated Members must be women.

(3) Members hold office for a period not exceeding three years.

(4) The NGO-nominated Member is paid fees/allowances as prescribed by the
employer.

(5) A Presiding Officer or Member may be removed for contravening Section 16,
being convicted of an offence, being found guilty in disciplinary
proceedings, or abusing their position.

================================================================
SECTION 9 — COMPLAINT OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT
================================================================

(1) An aggrieved woman may file a written complaint of sexual harassment to
the Internal Committee (or Local Committee, if no IC exists) within three
months of the incident, or the last incident in a series. The Committee may
extend this by up to three months for recorded reasons, if satisfied the
woman was prevented from filing earlier.
   - If she cannot make the complaint in writing, the Presiding Officer or
     any Member shall render reasonable assistance to help her file it in
     writing.

(2) If the aggrieved woman is unable to complain due to physical/mental
incapacity, death, or otherwise, her legal heir or another prescribed person
may file the complaint.

================================================================
SECTION 10 — CONCILIATION
================================================================

(1) Before initiating an inquiry, and at the request of the aggrieved woman,
the Committee may attempt to settle the matter through conciliation. No
monetary settlement shall be used as the basis of conciliation.

(2)-(3) Where settlement is reached, the Committee records it, forwards it to
the employer/District Officer, and provides copies to both parties.

(4) Where a settlement is reached, no further inquiry is conducted.

================================================================
SECTION 11 — INQUIRY INTO COMPLAINT
================================================================

(1) The Committee shall inquire into the complaint per applicable service
rules. Where the respondent is not an employee (e.g. involves a domestic
worker) and a prima facie case exists, the complaint is forwarded to police
within seven days for registration under Section 509 IPC (now Section 79,
BNS) or other relevant provisions.
   - If a prior conciliation settlement is breached, the Committee proceeds
     to inquiry or forwards the complaint to police.
   - Where both parties are employees, both get an opportunity to be heard
     and to review the findings before the Committee.

(2) The court may order the respondent to pay compensation to the aggrieved
woman upon conviction.

(3) For the inquiry, the Committee has powers similar to a civil court under
the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, including summoning witnesses and
requiring production of documents.

(4) The inquiry must be completed within ninety days.
